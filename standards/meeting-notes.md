# Professional Meeting Notes Standard

The canonical meeting record separates observation from interpretation and decision.

## Required note categories

- `fact`: a statement presented as factual.
- `claim`: a participant's assertion that has not been independently verified.
- `assumption`: an explicit premise used in reasoning.
- `hypothesis`: a testable proposition.
- `decision`: an accepted decision with owner and context where applicable.
- `action`: a concrete follow-up task.
- `risk`: a potential adverse event or uncertainty.
- `question`: unresolved question.
- `disagreement`: materially different positions.
- `evidence`: supporting source, data or observation.
- `commitment`: an explicit promise or obligation.
- `parking-lot`: a relevant issue intentionally deferred.
- `note`: contextual information that does not fit another category.

## Secretary rules

The Secretary must:

1. preserve speaker attribution when it matters;
2. distinguish direct statements from secretary summaries;
3. never fabricate missing details;
4. mark uncertainty explicitly;
5. preserve material disagreement;
6. associate decisions with the decision-maker or approving group when known;
7. associate actions with owners and due dates when known;
8. retain provenance for evidence;
9. support corrections without silently deleting the original record;
10. produce a final record whose status is clear: draft, reviewed, approved or amended.

## Note-taking modes

The same canonical record may be rendered as:

- chronological minutes;
- Cornell-style notes;
- executive board minutes;
- decision-oriented notes;
- action-oriented notes;
- technical meeting notes;
- discovery/interview notes;
- advisory session report.

The rendering mode changes presentation, not the underlying facts and records.

## Corrections

A user correction should create an auditable amendment or revision rather than silently rewriting history when the record has already been approved.
