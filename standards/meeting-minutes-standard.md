# Professional Meeting Minutes Standard

## Purpose

The Session Secretary produces a formal, structured record suitable for professional advisory meetings, review, follow-up and archival.

## Required document sections

1. Document header
2. Meeting identification
3. Date, time and status
4. Participants and roles
5. Meeting purpose
6. Agenda
7. Executive summary
8. Discussion by agenda item
9. Decisions
10. Action items
11. Risks
12. Assumptions
13. Open questions
14. Disagreements / dissent
15. Evidence and sources
16. Specialist contributions
17. Follow-up agenda
18. Closure
19. Approval status
20. Amendment history

## Writing rules

The Secretary must write objectively and professionally, use concise complete sentences, remove conversational noise and repetition, preserve material meaning, and distinguish observation from interpretation.

The Secretary must distinguish recommendation from decision and commitment from suggestion. Unresolved issues must remain visible.

The Secretary must never manufacture dates, owners, votes, approvals, consensus or evidence. Unknown information remains explicitly unknown or pending confirmation.

## Decision wording

A decision should identify, where available:

- decision ID;
- decision statement;
- decision owner / approving party;
- rationale;
- relevant evidence;
- affected agenda item;
- status.

A recommendation is not a decision until the authorized decision-maker confirms it.

## Action wording

Each action should contain:

- action ID;
- action description;
- owner;
- due date;
- status;
- originating decision or agenda item when available.

Unknown owner or due date must not be inferred.

## Disagreement handling

Material disagreement must not be removed to make minutes appear unanimous. Record the issue, competing positions, participants, cited evidence, current status and required follow-up.

## Approval and amendment

Every final document has one status: draft, reviewed, approved or amended.

Only the user or explicitly authorized approving party may move the document to approved.

Corrections to an approved record are amendments. The original approved version remains traceable.

## Output relationship

Minutes, executive summaries, decision logs, action registers and advisory reports are projections of the same canonical Meeting Record and must not introduce facts absent from it.
